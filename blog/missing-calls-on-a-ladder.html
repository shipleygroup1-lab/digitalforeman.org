I Was Missing Calls on a Ladder. Here's What It Was Costing Me.

By Tom Shipley · Owner of Simply Enclosures and founder of Digital Foreman · November 15, 2026

When you're the owner, you're also the installer, the estimator and the guy who answers the phone. The problem is that the phone always rings at the worst time: when you're up on a ladder, holding a panel in place, or have both hands full.

For a long time, I just let it ring. I figured I'd call them back when I got down.

What actually happens when you don't answer

Here's what I learned the hard way: most people don't leave a voicemail. They hang up and call the next contractor on Google. By the time I got off the ladder and called back, a lot of them had already talked to someone else.

When I finally paid attention, I was missing 10 to 20 calls a week on average. On busy days, it was 10 to 20 in a single day.

And I know for a fact I lost at least a handful of jobs just because I called back too late. Not because my price was wrong or my work wasn't good. Because somebody else answered first.

That's the part that stings. You spend money on ads, a website and business cards to make the phone ring, and then the job goes to whoever picks up.

What I use now

Today, if I can't pick up, an AI receptionist answers for me, 24 hours a day. Here's exactly what it does, and what it doesn't:

What it does:

  • Answers in my company's name when I'm busy or after hours.
  • Asks what the caller needs, their name, phone number, email and address.
  • Sends that straight into my customer system as a new lead, so I don't type anything.
  • Takes a message if it's something I need to handle myself.

What it doesn't do:

  • It doesn't book an appointment live on the call. (That's an upgrade.)
  • It doesn't replace me. It gets the person's info so I can call back with everything in front of me.
  • It's not perfect. Every so often someone calls about an old job, and I still have to sort that out myself.

The biggest change isn't the technology. Every call gets answered now. Things run a lot smoother. And here's what surprised me: once someone has talked to a real-sounding voice and knows I'll get back to them, customers are happy to wait for my call. They don't hang up and call the next guy.

What I'd tell any contractor

You don't need to start with AI. Start by finding out how big the leak is:

  1. Look at your phone's call log for last week. Count the calls you didn't answer.
  2. Count how many of those left a voicemail.
  3. Ask yourself: how many of the rest did you call back within an hour?

If the numbers bother you, fix it. Even a simple text that goes out automatically when you miss a call ("Sorry I missed you, I'm on a job. What can I help with?") is better than silence.
