Getting found on Google

My Contractor Website Wasn't on Google. Here's What Was Wrong.

By Tom Shipley · Owner of Simply Enclosures and founder of Digital Foreman · November 15, 2026

I build screened porches on the Grand Strand in South Carolina. I have a website, a Google Business Profile, Facebook ads that bring in leads every week, and a phone system that answers when I'm on a ladder.

So I figured I was showing up on Google.

I wasn't.

What I found when I actually checked

One Sunday morning I typed my own business name into Google. My Instagram showed up. An old Facebook post showed up. Three of my competitors' business profiles showed up right at the top.

My website didn't show up at all. Neither did my Google Business Profile.

So I dug in. Here's what was going on:

1. Google had zero pages of my website. You can check this yourself. Go to Google and type site: followed by your web address, with no space, like site:yourwebsite.com. If nothing comes back, Google doesn't have your site at all. That was me: zero results.

2. Google had visited my site months earlier and decided to skip it. Google's free Search Console tool told me the whole story: it looked at my home page back in May and chose not to add it. My booking page and my porch design page? Google didn't even know they existed.

3. My site had no sitemap. A sitemap is a simple list of your pages that tells Google, "here's everything on my site." Mine didn't have one.

4. My Business Profile was brand new and set up a little differently than my competitors'. It was verified and live in Google Maps, but it only had 2 reviews, and its main category didn't match what the businesses at the top were using.

None of this was broken in a way you could see. My website loaded fine. It looked good. Customers who had the link could book. But for anyone searching, I was basically invisible.

The fixes (most took minutes, not days)

Fix 1: I added a sitemap. It's one small file that lists your pages. If someone else built your website, ask them for it. It's a 10-minute job.

Fix 2: I set up Google Search Console and submitted the sitemap. Search Console is free. Once your site is connected, you paste in your sitemap address and Google confirms it found your pages. Mine said "Success, 3 pages."

Fix 3: I asked Google to index each page. Inside Search Console there's a "Request indexing" button. I clicked it for my home page, my booking page and my porch design page. Google puts them in a priority line to be checked.

Fix 4: I changed my Business Profile's main category to match what customers actually search for in my area. The old category moved to a secondary spot, so I didn't lose it.

Fix 5: I'm asking happy customers for reviews. Not a fancy campaign. Just a short text with a link right after a job is finished.

What I'd tell any contractor

Don't assume you're on Google because you have a website. Check it today. It takes 30 seconds:

  1. Google your business name. Do you show up, or only your competitors?
  2. Google site:yourwebsite.com. Does anything come back?
  3. Look at your Google Business Profile. How many reviews do you have compared to the businesses at the top?

If any of those answers surprise you, you're not alone. I'm a contractor too, and I didn't know until I looked.
