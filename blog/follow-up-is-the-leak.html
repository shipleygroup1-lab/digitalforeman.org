Follow-up and closing

Only 3 of My 145 Proposals Said No. The Rest Just Went Quiet.

By Tom Shipley · Owner of Simply Enclosures and founder of Digital Foreman · November 16, 2026

For a long time I thought my price was the problem. If a proposal didn't close, I figured they found somebody cheaper. Then I actually looked at the numbers in my CRM.

What the numbers said

Out of 145 proposals, only 3 customers told me no.

That's it. Three. Everybody else who didn't sign never said no. They just went quiet.

That changed how I saw the whole thing. People weren't turning me down. Most of them were busy, still thinking, waiting on a spouse, or waiting on money, and nobody followed up with them. Including me.

My closing ratio over the last 30 days was 36.4%. My goal is 45%.

What I did about it

One Monday morning, I picked out the 8 biggest recent proposals, about $104,000 sitting on the table, and I called every one of them. No script, nothing fancy. Just:

"Hey, it's Tom. Are you still planning the project? Is there anything about the proposal I can change for you?"

Then I marked each one in my CRM: won, lost, or "next spring." I also turned on automatic follow-up texts at 60 and 90 days for anyone who doesn't answer.

What happened that week

  • The week before: 1 job won, $778 in sales.
  • The week I made those calls: 6 jobs won, $19,673 in sales.
  • My closing ratio that week: 75%.

Was every one of those jobs because of a phone call? No, I can't prove that. But I can tell you the price didn't change. The work didn't change. The only thing I did differently was follow up.

What I'd tell any contractor

Before you drop your price, check this:

  1. Open your CRM (or your notebook) and count your open proposals from the last 90 days.
  2. Count how many actually told you no.
  3. Count how many you followed up with more than once.

If most of them never said no, your price probably isn't the problem. Your follow-up is. And follow-up is the cheapest fix there is: a phone call, or a friendly text that goes out on its own.
